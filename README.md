# Electric Vehicle Adoption Analysis – Sustainable Mobility & EV Planning

## Project Overview
This project analyzes official electric vehicle registration data using Power BI to extract
strategic insights for sustainable mobility planning, infrastructure development, and policy design.

The analysis is framed from the perspective of a Sustainable Mobility & EV Adoption Manager.

## Business Questions
1. How has EV adoption evolved over time?
2. What is the distribution between BEV and PHEV vehicles?
3. How does EV adoption vary across U.S. states?
4. Which manufacturers dominate the EV market?
5. Which EV models are most popular among consumers?

## Dataset
- Source: U.S. Government Open Data (data.gov)
- Dataset: Electric Vehicle Population Data
- Key fields: Vehicle Type, Model Year, Manufacturer, Model, State, Electric Range

## Tools & Technologies
- Power BI (Data modeling, DAX, dashboards)
- Excel (initial inspection & cleaning)

## Key Insights
- EV adoption shows strong acceleration post-2017, indicating transition to mass adoption.
- BEVs dominate the market (≈78%), signaling declining reliance on transitional hybrid technologies.
- EV adoption is geographically concentrated, with Washington state leading.
- Tesla dominates both manufacturer and model-level market share.
- Consumer demand is highly concentrated around a small number of EV models.

## Managerial Impact
- Supports charging infrastructure planning
- Enables region-specific policy and incentive design
- Helps identify strategic manufacturer partnerships
- Informs long-term energy and grid capacity planning

## Conclusion & Limitations
This project demonstrates how EV registration data can be analyzed using Power BI to generate insights for sustainable mobility planning. The analysis shows strong growth in EV adoption, increasing preference for Battery Electric Vehicles (BEVs), regional differences across states, and high market concentration among a few manufacturers and models.

The main limitation is that the dataset only includes vehicle registrations. It does not capture charging behavior, vehicle usage, or socioeconomic factors. Future work could combine this data with charging infrastructure, grid capacity, and demographic information to support more detailed and accurate decision-making.
